# Formualizer 0.10 vs 0.9.3 and LibreOffice

These are the numbers behind the 0.10.0 release notes and the README.

## Setup

- **Machine:** a shared 24-core AMD Ryzen 9 3900XT under Linux 6.17, with other workloads running. Arms were interleaved per workbook to cancel machine drift. Treat single-workbook ratios within about ±10% as noise.
- **Builds:** release builds of 0.9.3 (`362becff`) and 0.10 (`main` at the Program 3 merge, `8e31151c`).
- **0.9.3 has two arms:**
  - the default configuration;
  - FormulaPlane span evaluation (`with_span_evaluation(true)`), 0.9.3's opt-in fast path.
- **Harness:** `program1-perf` from `formualizer-bench-core`, driving the public `Workbook` API through the Calamine loader. For each workbook it measures:
  - load, and the first `evaluate_all`;
  - 20 value edits and 20 formula edits, each followed by `evaluate_all` (median per edit);
  - live heap after the first calculation, and the peak heap through it (counting allocator, in a separate pass);
  - peak RSS.
- **Modes:** ephemeral and interactive (deferred graph build). Timing ran with allocation counting off.
- **Corpora:**
  - the Enron spreadsheet sample (Hermans & Murphy-Hill), 50 files, of which 27 load in both versions;
  - two realistic finance and operations models, generated in the repository by `generate-corpus` (no third-party content);
  - 17 synthetic scenario workbooks;
  - a generated 1M-formula financial model.
- **End-to-end scenarios:** the 17 native scenarios in `benchmarks/scenarios.yaml` (`run-formualizer-native`), 3 rounds.
- **Values:** 0.10's values were verified separately against the previous engine, cell by cell, with a fixed clock, at the first calculation, after every edit and at the end.

Ratios are 0.10 / 0.9.3, geomean over the workbooks where both versions complete the first calculation. Lower is better.

Two notes on this table:
- **Span mode was slower to load.** Against 0.9.3's span mode, Enron load is 0.14 and first calculation 0.28 (0.10 in interactive mode); real-model load is 0.06.
- **Interactive load is not comparable on its own.** Interactive mode defers formula ingest to the first calculation, so compare load + first.

**Robustness.** 0.10 completes every workbook 0.9.3 completes, plus two it cannot:
- one Enron workbook that 0.9.3 did not finish in 15 minutes, which 0.10 calculates in 0.5 s;
- one that 0.9.3 fails to load, or errors on at first calculation.

**Where 0.10 is not faster.** No workbook is materially slower. The only ratios above 1.05 are sub-millisecond operations on two-formula and structural test workbooks.

## LibreOffice Calc

LibreOffice Calc 24.2.7.2, headless over UNO, threaded formula-group calculation, OpenCL off. Formualizer `main` `8e31151c` through the `probe-pattern-prep` cold-load probe (Calamine loader). One fresh process per sample for Formualizer, and a warm listener for LibreOffice, whose startup is excluded. 52 cases: 23 generated witness shapes (4k to 100k formulas), 17 synthetic scenario workbooks, 2 generated finance and operations models, and 10 Enron workbooks spread across the sample by formula count. 3 rounds, interleaved per case; medians.

LibreOffice compiles formulas while loading. Formualizer's `ephemeral` mode does the same, while `interactive` mode (the bindings' default) defers building the formula graph to the first calculation. Calculation alone is therefore compared in `ephemeral` mode. Load + first calculation is the same in both modes (0.24× overall).

LibreOffice is faster end to end in four cases:
- `irregular-100000` (1.47×) and `irregular-16384` (1.09×): every formula is unique, so nothing is shared, and LibreOffice's compiled token interpreter beats Formualizer's per-cell evaluation.
- `fanout_100k` (1.18×): 100k formulas that differ only by a literal, which Formualizer does not group.
- One Enron model at 1.02× (0.84× in interactive mode).

Calculation alone is also slower on some Enron workbooks dominated by unique formulas (up to 2.4×), and on two tiny sparse cases where both engines take under 6 ms.

The harness's value read-back (with the 0.9.3 Python wheel) differs from LibreOffice on the same 64 cells in 5 Enron workbooks as in every earlier run. None of those differences came from this release.
